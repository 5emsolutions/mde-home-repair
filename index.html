Decks, Porches & Exterior Structures

Decks, covered decks, screened porches, front porch rebuilds, pergolas, ramps and stairs. This is the work we do most, and it is the work most people do not realize a company called Home Repair and Paint does.

A deck fails from the ground up. Footings that were never deep enough, a ledger board bolted to the siding instead of the band, joist hangers with the wrong nails in them. We tear it back to whatever is still sound and rebuild from there. If the footings are good we say so and you save the money.

Most deck rebuilds run $6,000–$14,000. A covered deck with a real roof over it runs $18,000–$34,000. A screened porch runs $22,000–$45,000. Stairs and rail replacement on their own start around $1,400.

What that includes

  • Tear-out and haul-off of the old structure
  • Footings dug and poured to NC code depth, inspected before we build on them
  • Pressure-treated framing, hot-dip galvanized or stainless fasteners
  • Decking in pressure-treated pine, cedar or composite — your call, we price all three
  • Ledger board pulled off the siding and bolted to the band with proper flashing
  • Rails, stairs, skirting and post wraps
  • Permit pulled and inspection scheduled where the county requires it

What it does not include

  • Electrical for a hot tub or outdoor kitchen (licensed sub, we coordinate it)
  • Engineered drawings for elevated second-story decks — we bring in an engineer, billed at cost
  • Grading, drainage or landscaping around the deck
  • Stain or paint — we do it, it is a separate line so you can see the number
  • HOA architectural submissions

Roofing — Residential and Commercial Flat

Shingle reroofs, leak repairs, storm damage, structural roof framing, and full tear-off and EPDM replacement on flat commercial roofs.

Most roof calls we get are not really roof calls. Water shows up on a ceiling and the shingles are fine — it is a boot, a valley, a chimney flashing, or a nail pop from a windy day. We find where it actually comes in before anybody quotes you a whole roof. When the roof genuinely is done, we tell you that too.

A shingle reroof on a typical Eastern NC ranch runs $9,000–$16,000. Leak repairs run $450–$2,500. Commercial flat roof tear-off and new EPDM runs $8–$14 a square foot installed — so a 5,000 sq ft roof lands between $40,000 and $70,000.

What that includes

  • Tear-off down to the deck — we do not shingle over an existing roof
  • Deck inspected sheet by sheet; rotten sheathing replaced at a per-sheet price quoted up front
  • Synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in valleys and around every penetration
  • New drip edge, pipe boots, and ridge vent
  • Architectural shingles with the manufacturer warranty registered in your name
  • Magnet sweep of the yard and driveway, and every scrap hauled off
  • On flat roofs: full tear-off, new insulation board, fully adhered or mechanically fastened EPDM, all new flashing and edge metal

What it does not include

  • Full deck replacement beyond the sheet allowance — priced once it is open and we can see it
  • Gutters and gutter guards (we install them, separate line)
  • New skylight units, though we flash and set one you buy
  • Filing your insurance claim — we will meet your adjuster on the roof and document everything, but the claim is yours to file
  • Rafter or truss replacement, quoted separately as framing work

Full-room demo down to the joists, subfloor replacement, whole-house drywall hang and finish, flooring, and kitchen remodels.

Rot Repair, Siding, Soffit, Fascia & Trim

Soffit and fascia rot, siding repair and replacement, porch columns and posts, band boards, window and door trim, and the framing behind all of it.

This is the most common thing we get called for in this part of the state and the thing most often done badly. Painting over soft wood buys you eighteen months. We probe with an awl until we hit sound wood, cut back past it, and replace the framing underneath if the framing is gone too. Every board gets primed on all six sides before it goes up — including the cut ends, which is where rot starts again.

A section of soffit and fascia runs $800–$3,500. Fascia and soffit around a whole house runs $4,500–$11,000. Siding repair runs $1,200–$4,000. Full siding replacement runs $12,000–$28,000. A rotted porch column is $600–$2,000 each depending on whether it is holding anything up.

What that includes

  • Probing the whole run to find where the rot actually stops, not just where you can see it
  • Cutting back to sound wood and replacing rotted framing, not just the skin over it
  • New sheathing and house wrap where the siding comes off
  • Your choice of material: PVC trim, fiber cement, or wood
  • Every board primed on all six sides, cut ends included, before install
  • Flashing over every horizontal trim board, and joints caulked
  • Handed back paint-ready, or painted if you want it in the same trip

What it does not include

  • Termite treatment — if we find live activity we stop, show you, and you call a licensed exterminator
  • Gutter replacement (separate line, and often worth doing while the fascia is open)
  • Full house wrap behind siding that is staying on
  • Sill plate or band board replacement — real structural work, quoted once we open the wall
  • Window replacement, though we do the trim and flashing around new ones

Interior and Exterior Painting

Whole-house interiors, exterior repaints, ceilings, trim, doors, and porch floors. Half our name is Paint and we take the prep more seriously than the paint.

An exterior paint job in Eastern North Carolina lives or dies on prep. Our humidity and our summers will find every bare nail head and every unsealed end grain. So we wash it, scrape it, sand it, spot-prime every bare spot, and caulk the joints before a finish coat goes on. If there is rot under the paint we fix the rot first — we would rather tell you that in the estimate than discover it with a sprayer in our hand.

One interior room runs $450–$900. The whole interior of an 1,800 sq ft house runs $4,500–$9,500. An exterior repaint runs $4,500–$11,000, and more if there is rot to fix before we can paint it.

What that includes

  • Pressure washing, scraping and sanding — on exteriors this is most of the labor
  • Spot-priming every bare spot, and full priming on new or repaired wood
  • Caulking joints, nail holes filled, cracks bridged
  • Two finish coats, cut in by hand at the lines
  • Everything masked, furniture moved and covered, floors protected
  • Name-brand paint — Sherwin-Williams or Behr Marquee by default, or whatever you specify
  • Daily cleanup, and the job site left walkable every evening

What it does not include

  • Lead-safe containment on pre-1978 homes — required by law and quoted as its own line
  • Popcorn ceiling removal (we do it, separate line, it is a messy day)
  • Wallpaper removal
  • Cabinet spraying — done off-site in a booth for a real finish, priced separately
  • Deck and fence staining, which is priced by the square foot, not by the room

What a commercial roof actually costs you when it fails

It is rarely the roof. It is the tenant whose inventory got wet, the ceiling tile budget, the lease conversation that gets harder every time it rains, and the deck you now have to replace because the insulation stayed saturated for two years.

A flat roof gives you a lot of warning before it goes. Ponding that does not drain in 48 hours, seams lifting at the laps, blisters, a membrane that has gone chalky and hard, interior stains that move. Caught at that stage you often have a maintenance scope and a few more budget cycles. Caught after the insulation is soaked, you have a tear-off.

We take core cuts and show you which one you have. That is a short visit and it costs you nothing.

What we do

  • Full tear-off and replacement. Down to the deck, deck repaired where needed, new insulation board, fully adhered or mechanically fastened EPDM, all new edge metal, flashing and drain details.
  • Leak diagnostics and repair. Finding where water actually enters, which is almost never above where it appears inside.
  • Preventive maintenance. Seam and flashing inspection, drain clearing, resealing penetrations, and a written condition report you can hand to an owner.
  • Roof-over evaluation. Sometimes a recover is legitimate and saves you a third of the cost. Sometimes the code and the wet insulation say no. We will tell you which.
  • Membranes we work with. EPDM primarily, plus TPO and modified bitumen, and metal roofs on older commercial buildings.

Budget numbers

Tear-off and new EPDM runs $8–$14 per square foot installed across this market. Drivers are membrane thickness, adhered versus mechanically fastened, insulation R-value, how much deck has to be replaced, and access.

What building owners ask us

Can you work while the building is occupied?

Yes, and most of what we do is occupied. We phase the roof so only the section we can dry in that day is ever open, and we sequence loud work — tear-off, fastening — around your tenants’ hours where the schedule allows it. We do not need interior access except at penetrations and interior drains, and we give notice before we need it.

How do I know whether to repair or replace?

Age, saturation and how many times it has already been patched. If the insulation under the membrane is wet, patching the top is money spent twice — the wet board keeps the deck damp and the R-value is gone anyway. We take core cuts and show you what is under there before you commit to either number.

What warranty comes with a new membrane?

Manufacturer warranties on EPDM typically run 10, 15 or 20 years depending on membrane thickness and whether it is fully adhered. That covers the material. Our workmanship is warranted separately and in writing. We will lay out what each one actually covers before you sign, because they are not the same thing and the difference matters at year seven.

Our capital budget is set for this year. Can you work to a date?

We can, and we would rather build to your fiscal calendar than push you into a bad month. If the roof can hold with a maintenance scope until next cycle we will tell you that and price the holding work. If it cannot, we will tell you that too, with photos.

Do you handle leaks on roofs you did not install?

Yes. Leak diagnostics is a good bit of our commercial work. Finding the entry point on a flat roof is its own skill — water travels along the deck and shows up thirty feet from where it got in.

Need a number for next year’s budget?

We will measure it, take core cuts, and give you a written scope and a real figure. No charge, and no pressure to spend it this cycle.

What we run into on Farmville houses

Farmville has a downtown and a set of streets around it that are better than a town this size has any right to be. Big two-story frame houses from the tobacco years, wide porches, original wood siding, tall wood windows and a lot of trim detail. They photograph beautifully and they are honest work to maintain.

The problem with a house like that is that it needs painting every seven to ten years in this climate, and the moment it goes twelve, paint stops being the fix. Water gets past a failed caulk joint or a bare nail head, gets into the end grain of a trim board, and the board is gone from the inside while the paint on the outside still looks alright. So on a Farmville exterior we walk the whole house with an awl before we price anything. You get one number that covers replacing what is bad and painting all of it.

We prime every replacement board on all six sides, cut ends included. On a house with this much trim that is a slow afternoon and it is the difference between a paint job that lasts eight years and one that lasts three.

  • Porch floors and columns on deep wraparound porches

Farmville questions

My house is old. Do I have to deal with lead paint?

If it was built before 1978, probably yes, and it is a legal requirement, not an upsell. Lead-safe containment is its own line on our estimate so you can see exactly what it costs. Any contractor who does not mention it on a house that age is cutting a corner you will not see.

Can you repair my old wood windows instead of replacing them?

Often we can, and on a house like the ones on Main and Wilson it is usually the better answer. Reglazing, repairing the sash and repainting keeps the character and costs less than replacement units that will not match.

What we run into on Bethel houses

Bethel is small and it is off the main run between the bigger towns, which means most people here have a story about a contractor who quoted a job and never came back. We are about half an hour away and we take work here on purpose, including work that is too small for a bigger outfit to bother with.

The housing here is older and it is mostly modest — frame houses, brick ranches, and a fair number of homes that have had a roof or an addition put on at some point by somebody who was working fast. Where two roof planes meet at an addition is where we find leaks more often than anywhere else in this town. Valley flashing done with caulk instead of metal will hold for years and then let go all at once.

North Pitt County also holds water. The land is flat, the drainage is slow, and after a heavy week the crawlspaces stay wet. That turns into soft subfloor, rot at the sill, and mildew on the north side of exteriors that will bleed straight through fresh paint if it is not killed first. We wash and treat before we paint here, every time.

Most common calls in Bethel

  • Leaks where an addition roof ties into the original roofline
  • Valley and step flashing done with caulk instead of metal
  • Wet crawlspaces turning into soft subfloor and rotted sill
  • Mildew on north-facing exteriors that has to be killed before paint
  • Small repairs other companies will not drive out for
