What we run into on Tarboro houses

Tarboro has one of the largest historic districts in North Carolina and roofs to match. Steep pitches, multiple valleys, dormers, standing-seam metal on the oldest houses, and a lot of framing that predates anything you would call a truss. Those roofs need somebody who will get on them and look, not somebody who quotes off a satellite measurement.

The town also remembers water. Floyd in 1999 and Matthew in 2016 taught everybody here what happens when the Tar River comes up, and a lot of houses were repaired in a hurry afterward. We still find roofs from that era with shingles laid over old shingles, valleys that were never properly woven, and flashing that was caulked instead of replaced. That kind of work holds for a decade and then all fails in the same season.

If your ceiling has a stain on it, the first thing we do is find where the water actually enters — which is almost never directly above the stain. Sometimes that is a $600 fix. We would rather do the $600 fix and have you call us in eight years for the real roof.

Most common calls in Tarboro

  • Steep, multi-valley roofs on historic-district homes
  • Standing-seam and 5V metal roofs on older houses and outbuildings
  • Layered shingles from post-flood repairs that are now failing all at once
  • Chimney, valley and pipe-boot flashing that was caulked instead of replaced
  • Rafter and decking replacement where a leak went unnoticed long enough to rot framing

Tarboro questions

How fast can you get here for a leak?

Tarboro is about fifteen minutes from our shop in Macclesfield. If it is actively coming through a ceiling, call — we will get a tarp on it before we talk about anything else.

Will you meet my insurance adjuster?

Yes. We will get on the roof with them, point out what we found, and give you documentation and photos. We do not file the claim for you — that is yours — but you will not be up there by yourself trying to explain a flashing detail.
