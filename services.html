Decks, Porches & Exterior Structures

Decks, covered decks, screened porches, front porch rebuilds, pergolas, ramps and stairs. This is the work we do most, and it is the work most people do not realize a company called Home Repair and Paint does.

A deck fails from the ground up. Footings that were never deep enough, a ledger board bolted to the siding instead of the band, joist hangers with the wrong nails in them. We tear it back to whatever is still sound and rebuild from there. If the footings are good we say so and you save the money.

Most deck rebuilds run $6,000–$14,000. A covered deck with a real roof over it runs $18,000–$34,000. A screened porch runs $22,000–$45,000. Stairs and rail replacement on their own start around $1,400.

What that includes

  • Tear-out and haul-off of the old structure
  • Footings dug and poured to NC code depth, inspected before we build on them
  • Pressure-treated framing, hot-dip galvanized or stainless fasteners
  • Decking in pressure-treated pine, cedar or composite — your call, we price all three
  • Ledger board pulled off the siding and bolted to the band with proper flashing
  • Rails, stairs, skirting and post wraps
  • Permit pulled and inspection scheduled where the county requires it

What it does not include

  • Electrical for a hot tub or outdoor kitchen (licensed sub, we coordinate it)
  • Engineered drawings for elevated second-story decks — we bring in an engineer, billed at cost
  • Grading, drainage or landscaping around the deck
  • Stain or paint — we do it, it is a separate line so you can see the number
  • HOA architectural submissions

Roofing — Residential and Commercial Flat

Shingle reroofs, leak repairs, storm damage, structural roof framing, and full tear-off and EPDM replacement on flat commercial roofs.

Most roof calls we get are not really roof calls. Water shows up on a ceiling and the shingles are fine — it is a boot, a valley, a chimney flashing, or a nail pop from a windy day. We find where it actually comes in before anybody quotes you a whole roof. When the roof genuinely is done, we tell you that too.

A shingle reroof on a typical Eastern NC ranch runs $9,000–$16,000. Leak repairs run $450–$2,500. Commercial flat roof tear-off and new EPDM runs $8–$14 a square foot installed — so a 5,000 sq ft roof lands between $40,000 and $70,000.

What that includes

  • Tear-off down to the deck — we do not shingle over an existing roof
  • Deck inspected sheet by sheet; rotten sheathing replaced at a per-sheet price quoted up front
  • Synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in valleys and around every penetration
  • New drip edge, pipe boots, and ridge vent
  • Architectural shingles with the manufacturer warranty registered in your name
  • Magnet sweep of the yard and driveway, and every scrap hauled off
  • On flat roofs: full tear-off, new insulation board, fully adhered or mechanically fastened EPDM, all new flashing and edge metal

What it does not include

  • Full deck replacement beyond the sheet allowance — priced once it is open and we can see it
  • Gutters and gutter guards (we install them, separate line)
  • New skylight units, though we flash and set one you buy
  • Filing your insurance claim — we will meet your adjuster on the roof and document everything, but the claim is yours to file
  • Rafter or truss replacement, quoted separately as framing work

Interior Remodeling — Drywall, Flooring, Kitchens

Full-room demo down to the joists, subfloor replacement, whole-house drywall hang and finish, flooring, and kitchen remodels.

We hang and finish our own drywall. That matters more than it sounds like it should — a lot of remodelers sub it out and then have to live with whatever shows up under a raking light. We also do not leave you living in a construction site: floors get covered, HVAC returns get sealed off, and the dust stays in the room it came from.

Drywall hang and finish in one room runs $1,800–$4,000. LVP flooring installed runs $4.50–$8 a square foot. Subfloor replacement runs $1,800–$6,000 depending on how far the soft spot goes. A full kitchen runs $18,000–$45,000; a cosmetic kitchen refresh runs $8,000–$15,000.

What that includes

  • Demo and haul-off, down to the joists if that is what it takes
  • Floor protection, plastic containment, and HVAC returns sealed while we work
  • Framing changes to non-load-bearing walls
  • Drywall hung, taped, three coats, sanded to a Level 4 finish, texture matched to the rest of the house
  • Subfloor and underlayment, leveled before flooring goes down
  • Flooring: LVP, laminate, tile, or hardwood
  • Trim, base, casing, and cabinet setting

What it does not include

  • Appliances
  • Countertop fabrication and templating (subbed to a fabricator, priced separately so you see the real number)
  • Plumbing and electrical beyond like-for-like replacement — licensed subs, we schedule and manage them
  • Removing a load-bearing wall without an engineer’s letter
  • Asbestos or lead abatement — on a pre-1978 house we stop and tell you before we cut

Rot Repair, Siding, Soffit, Fascia & Trim

Soffit and fascia rot, siding repair and replacement, porch columns and posts, band boards, window and door trim, and the framing behind all of it.

This is the most common thing we get called for in this part of the state and the thing most often done badly. Painting over soft wood buys you eighteen months. We check the wood until we reach solid material, cut back past the rot, and replace the framing underneath if the framing is gone too. Every board gets primed on all six sides before it goes up — including the cut ends, which is where rot starts again.

A section of soffit and fascia runs $800–$3,500. Fascia and soffit around a whole house runs $4,500–$11,000. Siding repair runs $1,200–$4,000. Full siding replacement runs $12,000–$28,000. A rotted porch column is $600–$2,000 each depending on whether it is holding anything up.

What that includes

  • Checking the whole run to find where the rot actually stops, not just where you can see it
  • Cutting back to sound wood and replacing rotted framing, not just the skin over it
  • New sheathing and house wrap where the siding comes off
  • Your choice of material: PVC trim, fiber cement, or wood
  • Every board primed on all six sides, cut ends included, before install
  • Flashing over every horizontal trim board, and joints caulked
  • Handed back paint-ready, or painted if you want it in the same trip

What it does not include

  • Termite treatment — if we find live activity we stop, show you, and you call a licensed exterminator
  • Gutter replacement (separate line, and often worth doing while the fascia is open)
  • Full house wrap behind siding that is staying on
  • Sill plate or band board replacement — real structural work, quoted once we open the wall
  • Window replacement, though we do the trim and flashing around new ones

Interior and Exterior Painting

Whole-house interiors, exterior repaints, ceilings, trim, doors, and porch floors. Half our name is Paint and we take the prep more seriously than the paint.

An exterior paint job in Eastern North Carolina lives or dies on prep. Our humidity and our summers will find every bare nail head and every unsealed end grain. So we wash it, scrape it, sand it, spot-prime every bare spot, and caulk the joints before a finish coat goes on. If there is rot under the paint we fix the rot first — we would rather tell you that in the estimate than discover it with a sprayer in our hand.

One interior room runs $450–$900. The whole interior of an 1,800 sq ft house runs $4,500–$9,500. An exterior repaint runs $4,500–$11,000, and more if there is rot to fix before we can paint it.

What that includes

  • Pressure washing, scraping and sanding — on exteriors this is most of the labor
  • Spot-priming every bare spot, and full priming on new or repaired wood
  • Caulking joints, nail holes filled, cracks bridged
  • Two finish coats, cut in by hand at the lines
  • Everything masked, furniture moved and covered, floors protected
  • Name-brand paint — Sherwin-Williams or Behr Marquee by default, or whatever you specify
  • Daily cleanup, and the job site left walkable every evening

What it does not include

  • Lead-safe containment on pre-1978 homes — required by law and quoted as its own line
  • Popcorn ceiling removal (we do it, separate line, it is a messy day)
  • Wallpaper removal
  • Cabinet spraying — done off-site in a booth for a real finish, priced separately
  • Deck and fence staining, which is priced by the square foot, not by the room

Questions we get asked

Why do you publish prices when nobody else does?

Because the first thing you want to know is whether this is a $2,000 conversation or a $20,000 one, and making you sit through a sales visit to find out wastes your evening and our diesel. The ranges on this page are real numbers from real jobs in these counties.

Will the final price match the estimate?

It matches unless we open something up and find a problem that was not visible when we quoted — rotted framing behind siding, a bad subfloor under a good-looking floor. When that happens you get a call and a number before we do the extra work, never a surprise on the invoice.

Do you take on small jobs?

Yes. A rotted post, a section of fascia, one room of drywall. We would rather do the small job well and be the people you call when the big one comes.

Are you licensed and insured?

We are licensed and insured, and we will hand you the certificate of insurance before we start. We are also BBB Accredited with an A+ rating, which took a couple of years of finished jobs to earn.

Do you offer financing?

We do not finance in-house. For larger projects most folks use a home equity line or a personal loan through their own bank, and we are happy to give you a written scope your lender can work from.
